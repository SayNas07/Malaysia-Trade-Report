# Malaysian trade and external shocks

Long-run trade elasticities come from single-equation ARDL models on 2000Q1 to 2026Q2 (106 quarters). Shock dynamics come from a VECM on the same sample. The cointegrating relation estimated without commodity prices is dominated by the link between exports and imports: Malaysia imports intermediates and re-exports them, so imports absorb foreign demand and the foreign-GDP coefficient in that relation is not an export-demand elasticity. Export demand therefore excludes imports. Import demand includes exports. Log commodity prices (the IMF all-commodity index, PALLFNFINDEXQ) enter both demand equations and the VECM as an exogenous control, because dividing nominal trade by the consumer price index turns commodity-price swings into movements that look like volume. The GFC, COVID, and trade-war dummies are short-run regressors. The VECM uses the lag and rank selected for this file: 1 cointegrating relation and 0 lagged differences. No lag from 1 to 12 clears both residual-correlation tests once commodity prices are in the exogenous set. Lag 1 is the one with the higher minimum p-value (Portmanteau 0.602, Breusch-Godfrey 0.020). Malaysia is treated as a small open economy that does not move partner GDP.

The trade series is OpenDOSM monthly exports and imports of goods, in nominal ringgit, divided by Malaysia's headline CPI (division overall), summed across the three months of each complete quarter, then seasonally adjusted with STL. DOSM monthly goods trade starts in January 2000, so it is used on its own and Bank Negara nominal trade was not spliced. The CPI is a consumer price index, not a trade price or unit-value index, and the series is goods only, not national-accounts goods and services. Over the overlap with DOSM constant-2015 seasonally adjusted goods and services, the correlation of quarterly log growth is exports 0.895 (2015Q2 to 2026Q2, 45 growth quarters); imports 0.807 (2015Q2 to 2026Q2, 45 growth quarters). China GDP keeps the existing growth index through 2024Q1 and is then chained on NBS current-quarter preceding-year indices, where 104.3 means 4.3% above the same quarter a year earlier. Where the two overlap, the mean absolute gap between the index's implied year-on-year and the NBS index is 0.11 points, and the largest gap is +0.75 points in 2023Q2. The USD check converts the same nominal goods trade at EXMAUS and deflates by US CPI. CPIAUCSL is missing October 2025, so 2025Q4 is incomplete and that check stops before the gap.

## Long-run elasticities

Export demand is an ARDL with 1 lag of every variable: exports on foreign GDP, the REER, and commodity prices, with the GFC, COVID, and trade-war dummies in the short run only. Imports are excluded. The Pesaran bounds test (case 3, unrestricted intercept and no trend) has F = 1.61. The 5% critical bounds are 2.88 and 4.01, so the test does not reject no cointegration. The long-run elasticities are foreign GDP +0.89 (se 0.72, p=0.214); the REER +0.17 (se 1.66, p=0.919); commodity prices +0.23 (se 0.23, p=0.334). The error-correction coefficient is -0.095 (se 0.075, p=0.208) and is not a significant negative error correction at 5%. Breusch-Godfrey at 4 lags does not reject residual correlation (p=0.300). Jarque-Bera rejects normality (p=0.000). The equation uses 105 observations. Import demand is an ARDL with 1 lag of every variable: imports on exports, the REER, and commodity prices, with the same dummies in the short run only. The Pesaran bounds test (case 3, unrestricted intercept and no trend) has F = 4.11. The 5% critical bounds are 2.88 and 4.01, so the test rejects no cointegration. The long-run elasticities are exports +0.90 (se 0.20, p<0.001); the REER -0.48 (se 0.41, p=0.248); commodity prices +0.05 (se 0.09, p=0.549). The error-correction coefficient is -0.304 (se 0.076, p<0.001) and is negative and significant at 5%, so the levels error-correct. Breusch-Godfrey at 4 lags does not reject residual correlation (p=0.283). Jarque-Bera does not reject normality (p=0.059). The equation uses 105 observations.

## Shock dynamics

Commodity prices stay outside the cointegrating relation. Two placements of foreign GDP are still estimated. In the short-run specification it is an unrestricted regressor. In the restricted specification it is weakly exogenous: it enters the cointegrating relation only, lagged one quarter. TPU, commodity prices, and the episode dummies stay outside the relation in both. With foreign GDP outside the relation, the same-quarter elasticity of exports is 0.10 (p<0.001) and of imports is 0.15 (p<0.001). The largest companion root inside the unit circle has modulus 0.56.

Foreign GDP is restricted to the cointegrating relation. Normalised so the export weight is 1, the relation is exports = -4.21 (se 3.30, p=0.202) REER -1.27 (se 1.68, p=0.450) imports +0.16 (se 0.40, p=0.694) foreign GDP +61.35 (se 44.86, p=0.171). These are partial coefficients, holding the other variables in the relation fixed. The long-run partial elasticity of exports to foreign demand is 0.16 (p=0.694) and to the REER is -4.21 (p=0.202). The same vector, renormalised on imports, gives a long-run partial import elasticity of 0.12 to foreign demand (p=0.623) and -3.31 to the REER (p=0.083). Speeds of adjustment for the export-normalised equilibrium error are exports -0.134 (se 0.027, p<0.001); imports -0.165 (se 0.027, p<0.001); REER -0.023 (se 0.010, p=0.019). A negative export loading means an export level above the relation is pulled back down.

The same restricted VECM without commodity prices, on this sample and this lag, has a partial long-run export elasticity to foreign GDP of -0.46 (p=0.001), an import weight of 1.35, and a foreign-GDP export response of +0.00% on impact and +0.11% one quarter later. Its short-run export elasticity is 0.10 (p=0.030). Those are the figures the commodity-price control is being compared with. With commodity prices in the short-run equations the import coefficient in the solved relation is no longer that 1.35 processing-trade weight, and its standard error is large enough that the coefficient is not significant at 5%.

Scaled to a one-standard-deviation AR(1) innovation, the restricted-specification responses of exports are +0.00% on impact and +0.03% one quarter later for foreign GDP, -0.40% for the REER, and +0.84% for TPU. The matching import responses on impact are +0.00%, -0.40%, and +0.86%, and imports move +0.03% one quarter after the foreign-GDP innovation. The 90% bootstrap band for the export impact includes zero for foreign GDP, includes zero for the REER, and excludes zero for TPU. After the impact quarter the export band still excludes zero for the REER and TPU. Foreign GDP is zero on impact in this specification because it enters the cointegrating relation lagged one quarter. The foreign-GDP innovation has a standard deviation of 1.22%. The TPU innovation has a standard deviation of 53.8 index points. A positive REER shock is an appreciation.

Compared with the short-run specification, a 1 s.d. foreign-GDP innovation moves exports by +0.12% on impact (+0.01% at quarter 4). In the restricted specification the impact is +0.00% and the response one quarter later is +0.03% (+0.00% at quarter 4). The REER export impact is -0.15% in the short-run specification and -0.40% in the restricted one. The TPU export impact is +0.75% and +0.84%. Eight quarters ahead, the export forecast-error shares in the short-run specification are REER 9%, exports 74%, imports 18%. In the restricted specification they are REER 29%, exports 63%, imports 8%.

The forecast-error variance decomposition covers only shocks inside the Malaysian block. Foreign GDP and TPU are exogenous, so they get no share. On impact, export forecast errors are REER 1%, exports 99%, imports 0%. Eight quarters ahead the shares are REER 29%, exports 63%, imports 8%. Imports are ordered last in the Cholesky factor, so they cannot move exports in the impact quarter. The later import share is the permanent component of this ordering. It is not evidence that an import shock causes exports.

## Shock episodes

The episode estimates below are from the restricted VECM with commodity prices included. The GFC dummy shifts quarterly log-growth while it equals one. In percent, that shift is exports -11.8% (p<0.001, significant at 5%); imports -11.7% (p<0.001, significant at 5%); REER -1.8% (p=0.095, not significant at 5%). Error correction offsets a dummy that stays on, so these are not losses that compound quarter after quarter. The GFC counterfactual is a forecast from the model estimated before the episode (35 quarters), using the foreign path that actually happened. Actual exports minus that forecast are -10.2% in 2008Q4 and -10.2% in 2009Q2. Inside the window the gap ranges from -20.4% (2009Q1) to -10.2% (2009Q2). The COVID dummy shifts quarterly log-growth while it equals one. In percent, that shift is exports +0.9% (p=0.788, not significant at 5%); imports -3.9% (p=0.206, not significant at 5%); REER -1.2% (p=0.277, not significant at 5%). Error correction offsets a dummy that stays on, so these are not losses that compound quarter after quarter. The trade war dummy shifts quarterly log-growth while it equals one. In percent, that shift is exports +2.8% (p=0.060, not significant at 5%); imports +3.8% (p=0.009, significant at 5%); REER +0.3% (p=0.553, not significant at 5%). Error correction offsets a dummy that stays on, so these are not losses that compound quarter after quarter. The COVID counterfactual is a forecast from the model estimated before the episode (80 quarters), using the foreign path that actually happened. Actual exports minus that forecast are +4.6% in 2020Q1 and +46.2% in 2022Q4. Inside the window the gap ranges from -3.0% (2020Q2) to +56.9% (2022Q3). Both COVID forecasts run from 2020Q1 through 2022Q4. Over 2021Q1-2022Q4 the average gap between actual exports and the pre-COVID forecast is +37.2% when commodity prices follow their actual path and +74.5% when the same pre-COVID model omits commodity prices, so 50% of the no-commodity gap remains. The largest remaining gap in that window is +56.9% in 2022Q3. The trade war counterfactual is a forecast from the model estimated before the episode (74 quarters), using the foreign path that actually happened. Actual exports minus that forecast are -1.8% in 2018Q3 and -8.5% in 2019Q4. Inside the window the gap ranges from -8.5% (2019Q4) to +1.0% (2018Q4).

## Robustness

Jordà local projections of exports on the same 1 s.d. foreign-GDP innovation, with Newey-West standard errors, put the impact at +1.78% (the 90% band excludes zero) and the four-quarter response at +1.71%. The restricted VECM impact is +0.00% and the response one quarter later is +0.03%. Ending that specification in 2019Q4, the foreign-GDP export response one quarter later is -0.04% and the TPU export impact is +0.80%. The long-run export elasticity to foreign demand is -0.09 (p=0.141) on the sample that ends in 2019Q4, 0.45 (p=0.005) when trade is deflated by US CPI (through 2025Q3, 103 quarters), and 9.22 (p=0.314) for US GDP and -0.91 (p=0.300) for China GDP when those two replace the trade-weighted index inside the cointegrating relation. The matching import elasticities are 2.32 (p<0.001) for the US and -0.23 (p=0.031) for China.

## Diagnostics

The adjusted Portmanteau test at 4 lags does not reject residual autocorrelation at 5% (statistic 32.7, p=0.482). The Breusch-Godfrey LM test at the same lag rejects it (statistic 58.7, p=0.010). Jarque-Bera rejects normality (statistic 538.8, p=0.000). 2 roots lie on the unit circle, against 2 expected for rank 1 among 3 variables. The largest other modulus is 0.560. The residual bootstrap kept 500 of 500 draws. The log-likelihood is 638.5 on 105 estimation observations.

## What each model is for

The ARDL equations are the headline for long-run elasticities. The VECM is the model for shock dynamics: impulse responses, the forecast-error decomposition, and the episode forecasts. The export error-correction coefficient is not a significant negative adjustment, so the ARDL levels equation is not a confirmed long-run demand curve. The elasticities are still the ones to read, because the VECM foreign-GDP coefficient is partialled on imports. The VECM cointegrating vector still contains imports, so its foreign-GDP coefficient remains a partial association inside the processing-trade relation rather than an export-demand elasticity.

## Caveats

The estimation sample has 106 quarters. The deflator is the headline consumer price index, so the real trade series is not a constant-price national-accounts series. Log commodity prices are included so that a rise in commodity prices is not read as a rise in trade volume. The VECM still puts imports in the cointegrating relation, which is the processing-trade link, and that coefficient is not used as the export-demand elasticity. KPSS did not reject stationarity of the three endogenous series in levels, so the unit-root reading is the ADF result and is not unanimous. The REER impulse response is a Cholesky shock with the exchange rate ordered first: within a quarter, exports and imports do not move the REER. In the restricted specification, foreign GDP enters only through the lagged cointegrating relation, so its dynamic multiplier is zero in the quarter the innovation arrives. Foreign GDP and TPU are imposed to be exogenous rather than tested against a model in which Malaysia feeds back into them. Their multipliers are one-quarter impulses of the size of an AR(1) innovation (1.22% for foreign GDP, 54 index points for TPU), not the effect of a permanent rise in foreign demand. The trade-war variable is a step from 2018Q3, so it can absorb any shift in trade growth that lines up with that date. The GFC dummy varies in this sample. Bootstrap bands are conditional on the observed foreign path and use 500 successful redraws of the Malaysian residuals. Dummy p-values are asymptotic. COVID is both a dummy and a collapse in the foreign-GDP series, so those two channels are not cleanly separated.
