# Malaysian trade and external shocks

The estimates use a VECM on 2015Q1 to 2024Q1 (37 quarters). Log real exports, log real imports, and the log real effective exchange rate are endogenous. The system has one cointegrating relation and no lagged differences, the lag and rank selected for this file. Trade-weighted foreign GDP and trade-policy uncertainty are exogenous, because Malaysia is treated as a small open economy that does not move partner GDP. The COVID dummy and the US-China trade-war step sit outside the cointegrating relation as well.

## How sensitive trade was

The short-run elasticity of exports to trade-weighted foreign GDP is 1.33 (p<0.001). A 1% foreign-GDP innovation raises exports by about 1.33% in that quarter. Imports move with a short-run elasticity of 1.06 (p<0.001). Both responses die out within a couple of quarters. The third companion root has modulus 0.03, so a one-quarter impulse does not linger in this VECM.

Scaled to a one-standard-deviation AR(1) innovation, the impact responses of exports are +2.37% for foreign GDP, +0.66% for the REER, and -0.20% for TPU. The matching import responses are +1.89%, +0.26%, and -0.09%. The 90% bootstrap band for the export impact excludes zero for foreign GDP, includes zero for the REER, and includes zero for TPU. After the impact quarter the bands include zero. The foreign-GDP innovation in this sample has a standard deviation of 1.76% because the COVID quarter is in the residual. The TPU innovation has a standard deviation of 28.7 index points. A positive REER shock here is an appreciation. Its point estimate does not show the usual loss of export competitiveness, and the band includes zero.

Scaled so that exports have a weight of one, the cointegrating relation is exports = 0.21 REER + 0.56 imports -6.71. Exports enter the estimated residual with a negative weight and the export adjustment coefficient is positive (+0.38, p<0.001), so an export level above that relation is pulled back down. Import adjustment is +0.30 (p<0.001). The REER loading is +0.02 (p=0.443) and is not distinguishable from zero.

## Which external factor matters most

On the full-sample point estimates, foreign GDP moves exports by more than the REER or TPU. That ranking does not survive the sample that ends in 2019Q4: the same 1 s.d. foreign-GDP multiplier is then -0.02%, on an innovation of only 0.28% rather than 1.76%. Splitting partners, a 1 s.d. US GDP innovation moves exports by +2.58% on impact, while a 1 s.d. China GDP innovation moves them by -0.26%. The full-sample foreign-demand result is a US and COVID result, not a China result, and it is not visible in the pre-COVID window.

The forecast-error variance decomposition covers only shocks inside the Malaysian block. Foreign GDP and TPU are exogenous, so they get no share. On impact, export forecast errors are REER 4%, exports 96%, imports 0%. Eight quarters ahead the shares are REER 4%, exports 22%, imports 75%. Imports are ordered last in the Cholesky factor, so they cannot move exports in the impact quarter. The later import share is the permanent component of this ordering in a system with two unit roots. It is not evidence that an import shock causes exports.

## Shock episodes

The GFC dummy is zero in every quarter from 2015Q1 to 2024Q1, so it has no coefficient and there is no pre-2008 window for a counterfactual. The COVID dummy shifts quarterly log-growth while it equals one. In percent, that shift is exports -6.5% (p=0.002, significant at 5%); imports -7.8% (p=0.001, significant at 5%); REER -0.9% (p=0.465, not significant at 5%). Error correction offsets a dummy that stays on, so these are not losses that compound quarter after quarter. The trade war dummy shifts quarterly log-growth while it equals one. In percent, that shift is exports -3.8% (p=0.003, significant at 5%); imports -2.8% (p=0.044, significant at 5%); REER -0.3% (p=0.644, not significant at 5%). Error correction offsets a dummy that stays on, so these are not losses that compound quarter after quarter. The COVID counterfactual is a forecast from the model estimated before the episode (20 quarters), using the foreign path that actually happened. Actual exports minus that forecast are -2.9% in 2020Q1 and +37.0% in 2021Q4. Inside the window the gap ranges from -15.7% (2020Q2) to +37.0% (2021Q4). The trade war counterfactual is a forecast from the model estimated before the episode (14 quarters), using the foreign path that actually happened. Actual exports minus that forecast are -3.3% in 2018Q3 and -16.6% in 2019Q4. Inside the window the gap ranges from -16.6% (2019Q4) to -3.3% (2018Q3).

## Robustness

Jordà local projections of exports on the same 1 s.d. foreign-GDP innovation, with Newey-West standard errors, put the impact at +4.71% (the 90% band excludes zero) and the four-quarter response at +4.34%. The VECM impact is +2.37% and is essentially zero by quarter 4. The two methods agree that the full-sample impact is positive. They do not agree on how long it lasts. The local projection also uses the COVID quarter, so its tight band is not separate evidence from the VECM. Ending the VECM in 2019Q4 removes the foreign-GDP effect. Over that shorter sample the TPU impact on exports is +0.64%, the opposite sign from the full-sample point estimate.

## Diagnostics

The adjusted Portmanteau test at 4 lags does not reject residual autocorrelation at 5% (statistic 47.3, p=0.051). The Breusch-Godfrey LM test at the same lag rejects it (statistic 73.1, p=0.000). Jarque-Bera does not reject normality (statistic 8.5, p=0.204). 2 roots lie on the unit circle, against 2 expected for one cointegrating relation among three variables. The largest other modulus is 0.027. The residual bootstrap kept 500 of 500 draws. The log-likelihood is 253.8 on 36 estimation observations.

## Caveats

The estimation sample has 37 quarters. A VECM with three endogenous variables, two dummies, and two foreign regressors is a lot of structure for that length, and the information criteria only agree on one lag once lags that exhaust the degrees of freedom are set aside. KPSS did not reject stationarity of the three endogenous series in levels, so the unit-root reading is the ADF result and is not unanimous. The REER impulse response is a Cholesky shock with the exchange rate ordered first: within a quarter, exports and imports do not move the REER. Foreign GDP and TPU are imposed to be exogenous rather than tested against a model in which Malaysia feeds back into them. Their dynamic multipliers are one-quarter impulses of the size of an AR(1) innovation (1.76% for foreign GDP, 29 index points for TPU), not the effect of a permanent rise in foreign demand. The trade-war variable is a step from 2018Q3, so it can absorb any shift in trade growth that lines up with that date. The GFC is outside the sample. Bootstrap bands are conditional on the observed foreign path and use 500 successful redraws of the Malaysian residuals. Dummy p-values are asymptotic. COVID is both a dummy and a collapse in the foreign-GDP series, so those two channels are not cleanly separated.
