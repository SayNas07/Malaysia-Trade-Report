# Malaysian trade and external shocks

The estimates use a VECM on 2000Q1 to 2026Q2 (106 quarters). Log CPI-deflated goods exports, log CPI-deflated goods imports, and the log real effective exchange rate are endogenous. The lag and rank are those selected for this file: 1 cointegrating relation and 0 lagged differences. Two placements of foreign GDP are estimated. In the short-run specification it is an unrestricted regressor, outside the cointegrating relation. In the restricted specification it is weakly exogenous: it enters the cointegrating relation only, lagged one quarter, and it has no equation of its own. TPU and the GFC, COVID, and trade-war dummies stay outside the relation in both. Malaysia is treated as a small open economy that does not move partner GDP.

The trade series is OpenDOSM monthly exports and imports of goods, in nominal ringgit, divided by Malaysia's headline CPI (division overall), summed across the three months of each complete quarter, then seasonally adjusted with STL. DOSM monthly goods trade starts in January 2000, so it is used on its own and Bank Negara nominal trade was not spliced. The CPI is a consumer price index, not a trade price or unit-value index, and the series is goods only, not national-accounts goods and services. Over the overlap with DOSM constant-2015 seasonally adjusted goods and services, the correlation of quarterly log growth is exports 0.895 (2015Q2 to 2026Q2, 45 growth quarters); imports 0.807 (2015Q2 to 2026Q2, 45 growth quarters). China GDP keeps the existing growth index through 2024Q1 and is then chained on NBS current-quarter preceding-year indices, where 104.3 means 4.3% above the same quarter a year earlier. Where the two overlap, the mean absolute gap between the index's implied year-on-year and the NBS index is 0.11 points, and the largest gap is +0.75 points in 2023Q2. The USD check converts the same nominal goods trade at EXMAUS and deflates by US CPI. CPIAUCSL is missing October 2025, so 2025Q4 is incomplete and that check stops before the gap.

## Short-run specification

With foreign GDP outside the cointegrating relation, the short-run elasticity of exports is 0.10 (p=0.030) and the short-run elasticity of imports is 0.23 (p<0.001). These are same-quarter coefficients. They are not long-run trade elasticities. The largest companion root inside the unit circle has modulus 0.56.

## Restricted specification

Foreign GDP is restricted to the cointegrating relation. Normalised so the export weight is 1, the relation is exports = +0.20 (se 0.04, p<0.001) REER +1.35 (se 0.13, p<0.001) imports -0.46 (se 0.14, p=0.001) foreign GDP -2.42 (se 1.48, p=0.103). These are partial coefficients, holding the other variables in the relation fixed. The long-run partial elasticity of exports to foreign demand is -0.46 (p=0.001) and to the REER is 0.20 (p<0.001). The same vector, renormalised on imports, gives a long-run partial import elasticity of 0.34 to foreign demand (p<0.001) and -0.15 to the REER (p<0.001). Speeds of adjustment for the export-normalised equilibrium error are exports +0.195 (se 0.098, p=0.046); imports +0.478 (se 0.092, p<0.001); REER -0.018 (se 0.033, p=0.583). The export loading is positive.

Scaled to a one-standard-deviation AR(1) innovation, the restricted-specification responses of exports are +0.00% on impact and +0.11% one quarter later for foreign GDP, +0.18% for the REER, and +0.51% for TPU. The matching import responses on impact are +0.00%, +0.39%, and +0.67%, and imports move +0.27% one quarter after the foreign-GDP innovation. The 90% bootstrap band for the export impact includes zero for foreign GDP, includes zero for the REER, and excludes zero for TPU. After the impact quarter the export band still excludes zero for foreign GDP. Foreign GDP is zero on impact in this specification because it enters the cointegrating relation lagged one quarter. The foreign-GDP innovation has a standard deviation of 1.22%. The TPU innovation has a standard deviation of 53.8 index points. A positive REER shock is an appreciation.

Compared with the short-run specification, a 1 s.d. foreign-GDP innovation moves exports by +0.12% on impact (+0.01% at quarter 4). In the restricted specification the impact is +0.00% and the response one quarter later is +0.11% (+0.02% at quarter 4). The REER export impact is +0.19% in the short-run specification and +0.18% in the restricted one. The TPU export impact is +0.41% and +0.51%. Eight quarters ahead, the export forecast-error shares in the short-run specification are REER 0%, exports 92%, imports 8%. In the restricted specification they are REER 0%, exports 93%, imports 6%.

The forecast-error variance decomposition covers only shocks inside the Malaysian block. Foreign GDP and TPU are exogenous, so they get no share. On impact, export forecast errors are REER 0%, exports 100%, imports 0%. Eight quarters ahead the shares are REER 0%, exports 93%, imports 6%. Imports are ordered last in the Cholesky factor, so they cannot move exports in the impact quarter. The later import share is the permanent component of this ordering. It is not evidence that an import shock causes exports.

## Shock episodes

The episode estimates below are from the restricted specification. The GFC dummy shifts quarterly log-growth while it equals one. In percent, that shift is exports -11.8% (p=0.000, significant at 5%); imports -14.1% (p=0.000, significant at 5%); REER -1.5% (p=0.191, not significant at 5%). Error correction offsets a dummy that stays on, so these are not losses that compound quarter after quarter. The GFC counterfactual is a forecast from the model estimated before the episode (35 quarters), using the foreign path that actually happened. Actual exports minus that forecast are -16.4% in 2008Q4 and -12.4% in 2009Q2. Inside the window the gap ranges from -26.3% (2009Q1) to -12.4% (2009Q2). Error correction offsets a dummy that stays on, so these are not losses that compound quarter after quarter. The trade war dummy shifts quarterly log-growth while it equals one. In percent, that shift is exports -0.0% (p=0.999, not significant at 5%); imports -0.9% (p=0.505, not significant at 5%); REER -0.1% (p=0.859, not significant at 5%). ## Robustness

Jordà local projections of exports on the same 1 s.d. foreign-GDP innovation, with Newey-West standard errors, put the impact at +3.02% (the 90% band excludes zero) and the four-quarter response at +3.07%. The restricted VECM impact is +0.00% and the response one quarter later is +0.11%. Ending that specification in 2019Q4, the foreign-GDP export response one quarter later is +0.10% and the TPU export impact is +0.56%. The long-run export elasticity to foreign demand is -0.60 (p<0.001) on the sample that ends in 2019Q4, -0.48 (p<0.001) when trade is deflated by US CPI (through 2025Q3, 103 quarters), and -1.74 (p=0.008) for US GDP and 0.03 (p=0.654) for China GDP when those two replace the trade-weighted index inside the cointegrating relation. The matching import elasticities are 1.08 (p<0.001) for the US and -0.02 (p=0.648) for China.

## Diagnostics

The adjusted Portmanteau test at 4 lags does not reject residual autocorrelation at 5% (statistic 23.1, p=0.901). The Breusch-Godfrey LM test at the same lag does not reject it (statistic 40.7, p=0.271). Jarque-Bera rejects normality (statistic 99.6, p=0.000). 2 roots lie on the unit circle, against 2 expected for rank 1 among 3 variables. The largest other modulus is 0.556. The residual bootstrap kept 500 of 500 draws. The log-likelihood is 635.9 on 105 estimation observations.

## Which specification is preferred

The short-run specification is preferred. The restricted relation does not give a positive long-run export elasticity to foreign demand that is significant at 5%, so it is not a usable long-run export-demand curve. The short-run regression is the one that describes the quarterly association. The restricted estimates are still reported, because that is the specification in which a long-run elasticity would have been identified. The short-run export elasticity reported above is a same-quarter coefficient, not the long-run trade elasticity.

## Caveats

The estimation sample has 106 quarters. The deflator is the headline consumer price index, so the real trade series is not a constant-price national-accounts series. KPSS did not reject stationarity of the three endogenous series in levels, so the unit-root reading is the ADF result and is not unanimous. The REER impulse response is a Cholesky shock with the exchange rate ordered first: within a quarter, exports and imports do not move the REER. In the restricted specification, foreign GDP enters only through the lagged cointegrating relation, so its dynamic multiplier is zero in the quarter the innovation arrives. Foreign GDP and TPU are imposed to be exogenous rather than tested against a model in which Malaysia feeds back into them. Their multipliers are one-quarter impulses of the size of an AR(1) innovation (1.22% for foreign GDP, 54 index points for TPU), not the effect of a permanent rise in foreign demand. The trade-war variable is a step from 2018Q3, so it can absorb any shift in trade growth that lines up with that date. The GFC dummy varies in this sample. Bootstrap bands are conditional on the observed foreign path and use 500 successful redraws of the Malaysian residuals. Dummy p-values are asymptotic. COVID is both a dummy and a collapse in the foreign-GDP series, so those two channels are not cleanly separated.
